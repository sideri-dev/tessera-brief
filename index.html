Kyoto's 古裂會 (Kogire-kai) just published lot-by-lot results where four 李朝 (Joseon-dynasty Korean) paintings blew past reserve while a comparable 高麗 (Goryeo) ceramic barely cleared it.

The house's own results PDF for its 150th sale (September 17, 2026) names clean 落札価格 (hammer-price) figures against each lot's 最低価格 (reserve): a landscape hanging scroll cleared at ¥5,782,006 against a ¥400,000 reserve (14.46x), a silk-weaving-interior painting cleared at ¥3,756,432 against ¥100,000 (37.56x), and two more 李朝 paintings cleared at 7.34x and 6.63x. Against that, a 高麗青磁象嵌 (Goryeo celadon inlay) cup barely cleared its ¥600,000 reserve at ¥725,000 (1.21x) — the house names no buyer's premium or tax basis anywhere in the document, an open caveat worth carrying into any comparison.

A dated, typed clearing-price set direct from the primary results page — the strongest sourcing-lane item this corpus has run in weeks.

チヨダウーテ (Chiyoda Ute) has solved recycled gypsum's usual mixing-ratio ceiling — its CHIYODA CIRCULAR BOARD is now 100% recycled and still carries a standard-route fire certification.

Ordinary recycled gypsum tops out around 10% recycled content in the mix; the maker's own product pages describe a proprietary continuous-crystal-enlargement process that lets it reuse 100% of the waste gypsum board it processes. The 12.5mm board qualifies as 不燃材料 (non-combustible) under 建設省告示第1400号 and the 9.5mm board as 準不燃材料 (quasi-non-combustible) under 告示第1401号, both via the 告示仕様 standard-specification route rather than a product-specific 認定番号, and it ships nationwide.

A genuinely new-to-corpus, certified, fully recycled substitute for standard gypsum board wherever recycled content matters to a brief.

東リ (Toli) is raising its rubber-tile flooring prices 16.5–18.2% from next month's shipments.

The maker's own 上代 (list-price) revision table shows プラート moving from ¥11,000/㎡ to ¥13,000/㎡ — an 18.2% rise — and プラートF from ¥12,100/㎡ to ¥14,100/㎡, up 16.5%, both figures 税抜き (tax-excluded) and effective for shipments from October 21, 2026. Four new SKUs land alongside it — トワレタイルNW-EX at ¥28,000, バスナリアルデザイン at ¥10,900, a dust-control carpet at ¥18,000, and a low-odor cement at ¥35,000 per 16kg can — none of the new flooring or carpet SKUs states a 認定番号, so treat certification as open [CERT-UNKNOWN].

A dated per-square-metre price rise landing next month — direct input to any live bill of quantities still carrying the old Toli price.

TOFROM YAESU TOWER opens with its 68 shops facing the street instead of an internal atrium — and the design credit first reported for it was wrong.

The developer's own release states the plan deliberately rejects a circulation-driven mall layout, putting most of its 68 shops on the street frontage in a stacked "箱積み" (box-pile) massing meant to echo Yaesu's older small-building streetscape; the tower runs 51 storeys above ground and 4 below, about 250m tall, roughly 225,000㎡ total floor area, and opened September 10, 2026. Custom hinoki furniture is planned for the roughly 6,000㎡ public zone.

Corrected credit: basic design is 日本設計 (Nihon Sekkei), implementation design is 大林組 (Obayashi), construction is an 大林組/大成建設 (Obayashi/Taisei) joint venture — not the credit first reported for it. A correctly attributed, well-corroborated precedent for street-facing retail massing over inward-atrium circulation.

HNAA コンサルティング has put an actual yen figure on how much more Japan's current architecture fee standard (告示8号) costs than the one it replaced (旧98号).

A worked example runs a single 4-storey, 600㎡ tenant building through three budget assumptions: a flat 6%-of-construction-cost planning figure came to ¥9,000,000; recalculating under 旧告示98号 came to about ¥13,000,000; recalculating the same job under the current 令和6年告示第8号 came to about ¥16,000,000 — roughly 23% higher than the old standard and about 78% higher than the flat-percentage assumption.

Treat the yen figures as illustrative, since this is a private consultancy's own worked example rather than MLIT's own worksheet, but it's a citable, quotable number for anyone still anchored to the old standard or a flat percentage.

Last dossier

The corpus's deepest look yet at paying for Southeast Asian work from Japan finds both tax treaties are dead weight on a royalty: the Japan–Thailand cap (15%) and the Japan–Singapore cap (10%) each exactly match that treaty's own domestic royalty rate, so the only real lever is whether a design fee is contracted as a service or a licence. Thailand is harsher on every other axis too — a 3-month supervisory permanent-establishment threshold against Singapore's 6, a 6-month services threshold where Singapore has none, and a personal-services exemption that fails outright once the payer is a Thai client rather than a Japan resident.

Licensing bites before tax does: Thai interior work on public buildings of 500m² or more (hotels named) requires Thai domicile and a work permit with no ASEAN route in, while Singapore leaves interior design itself unregulated but voids an unauthorised supplier's fee entirely once a submitted plan is involved. On the money itself, a THB-denominated fee's yen value swung 22.84% peak-to-trough across 24 months (SGD 17.95%), and any tax withheld abroad beyond what the treaty actually permits is non-creditable back in Japan — relief has to be claimed at source, before payment, or it is gone.
