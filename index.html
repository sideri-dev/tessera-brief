Kansai International Airport Terminal 1's renovation has a confirmed four-way scope split — a live precedent for separating commercial fit-out from master planning on a large project.

Renzo Piano's KIX Terminal 1 is mid-way through a five-year renovation (工期 2021年6月1日〜2026年10月31日), targeting a 25% increase in international-terminal building area, six more international gate spots (34→40), and a 60% increase in departure-area space, across 613,436㎡ total floor area.

Two independently raw-fetched primary sources confirm the full credit split: POPULOUS holds master planning, basic design and design supervision; Nikken Sekkei holds basic/detailed design and structure; Obayashi holds detailed design and building/MEP execution; and 丹青社 (Tanseisha) holds the commercial-area interior fit-out specifically — a clean, citable model for separating the commercial-interior specialist from the architect-of-record and structural/services team.

The next edition of Japan's standard design-and-supervision contract (令和8年6月版) adds a customer-harassment clause — worth knowing before the next negotiation.

The 四会連合協定 standard contract used across Japanese architecture practice confirmed its June 2026 edition adds provisions on customer harassment and disruptive-client conduct, leaving the basic mutual-obligation structure unchanged. Two independent Japanese sources confirm the wording.

A small but citable clause for a live contract conversation, and a reminder that this standard revises roughly every five to six years (prior edition 2020) — the same edition-dating trap already tracked here for the government fee standard.

Singapore's DesignSingapore GDRD grant offers up to 50% co-funding for design R&D — but its hospitality/tourism eligibility is not actually stated anywhere on the primary page.

The funding mechanism is confirmed directly: up to S$50,000 for 12-month Design Research, up to S$450,000 for 24-month Design Development, or up to S$500,000 for a combined 36-month track, each co-funded up to 50% of qualifying costs.

What isn't there: no mention of "hospitality," "tourism," or eligibility criteria beyond general alignment with Singapore's Design 2035 Masterplan. The hospitality framing in circulation elsewhere rests on secondary sources only — treat the funding ceiling as confirmed and the hospitality fit as unverified until a project officer confirms it.

Last dossier

A deep read of プロポーザル and design-competition routes in Japan found the real gate is admission, not effort: 70.6% of local new-build design commissions still go to price competition, プロポーザル gets 19.5%, and open design competitions ran just twice. Where a proposal does run, the eligibility bar is a same-typology prime-contractor track record, scored at only 0–10% of the deciding total.

One city's own survey of 152 first-stage entrants found a median 40 person-days of submission labour — roughly ¥2.1–2.8M per entrant — against a ¥300,000 honorarium paid to only four of them (2.6% of entrants, nothing to the rest), and the winner usually had the worse published price score. The national guideline still grounds its no-reference-estimate rule in the design-fee standard superseded in January 2024 — twenty months of non-revision in the document governing how public design work is bought.
