A 24-year-old Kuma Kengo museum roof just failed in public, and the fix is a costed case against undersized exterior softwood.

The Bato Hiroshige Museum's thin cedar roof louvers — treated with water-soluble boron, which doesn't survive weather exposure — degraded over 24 years and have now been replaced with 30×60mm aluminum louvers, completed this March at roughly ¥300m. Kuma's own firm is on record with the argument that matters for a spec decision: aluminum costs more upfront than timber, but lifecycle cost, including maintenance, crosses over in aluminum's favor on an exposed façade. A ¥10m furusato-tax crowdfunding campaign aimed at offsetting the repair raised just ¥20,000 from two supporters; the renovation went ahead regardless. One correction worth carrying: the architect didn't stonewall questions about this renovation — that "declining comment" quote is from a 2024 interview request on an earlier article, and the 2026 report on the actual repair is built on direct interviews with Kuma's own staff.

A CLT floor plate, not beams, is what makes an irregular-plan sauna buildable without angled joinery.

OND SAUNA, a small built project in Saga by AMANE ARCHITECTS, uses a 150mm CLT floor panel to carry the structure, eliminating beams and, with them, every non-right-angle structural joint an obtuse, faceted plan would otherwise force. Independently confirmed on the architect's own figures and a second outlet: angled joinery is where small-footprint, faceted hospitality builds bleed labor cost, and a thick floor plate avoids it entirely rather than specing around it.

Obayashi's carbon-negative concrete has moved from product sheet to an actual pour — first civil-engineering cast-in-place use.

クリーンクリートN®, a blast-furnace-slag mix with a CO2-absorbing filler (partly sourced from captured CO2 off Asahi's vending-machine carbon-capture units), cuts CO2 emissions by up to roughly 120% on Obayashi's own figures and has now been cast at a Fukushima interim-storage foundation. No 不燃/準不燃/JIS certification is stated on the page — treat that route as unconfirmed before specifying it indoors — but the carbon-claim is usable now for exposed or board-formed concrete finishes where a client wants a defensible number.

Demolition window glass is now being turned back into float glass at commercial scale — a real supply route, not a pilot.

Obayashi, AGC and MUFG Bank recovered 149 tonnes of waste window glass demolishing MUFG's Tokyo HQ; 81 tonnes was horizontally recycled into new float glass at AGC's Kashima plant, cutting CO2 by 49 tonnes — Japan's first operational, non-pilot use of demolition glass as float-glass feedstock. That means a supplier you can call about a recycled-content glazing spec today. Fire/building certification for the recycled-content product itself isn't addressed in the release — confirm separately before specifying.

MLIT's actual renovation-fee costing method lives in its own 積算要領, built from a 93-office survey of 224 renovation cases, not derived from 告示第8号 itself. The widely-repeated "+8.2%" figure is a labour-hour coefficient, not a fee increase: the 2024 revision replaced a sliding formula with a flat ratio, which actually cuts the bundled fee below roughly 14 drawing sheets — the scale this practice typically works at — and raises it only above that. The per-sheet rate buys bare drafting only; basic design, 確認申請 and as-built survey are each separately chargeable, and renovation supervision has no formula at all. MLIT's own housing-policy review (Dec 2025) recommends scrapping the uniform renovation benchmark as unsuited to individualized work, the same year the building-works division revised that same benchmark upward — both positions stand, unresolved. The method carries no legal obligation to use it and is licensed at the user's own risk.
