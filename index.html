ROCKWOOL Japan's wrap-type fire cladding just cleared a new 1-hour certification for steel columns, not beams.

ROCKWOOL Japan's box-wrap "Tekkaroll" fireproofing earned 認定番号 FP060CN-1163, a 1-hour fire-rated composite-structure certificate for H-section steel columns ≥150×150×7×10mm — corrected here from the manufacturer's own page title, which said beams even though the body text is clearly about columns. A named, in-production, cleaner alternative to sprayed fire coatings, a class this corpus has already caught losing certification twice. Dated February 2026, eight months outside the usual window, but a 認定番号 is a durable fact until revoked, not news; single-sourced to ROCKWOOL's own newsroom. A wrong fire spec is a code failure, not an aesthetic one.

A Shinkansen's retired aluminum is now a certified window-sash material, already built into a 30-unit Tokyo condo.

"Re ALumi T50" (JR東海／三協立山／JR東海商事) uses 50% reclaimed Tokaido Shinkansen aluminum, cuts production CO2 ~40% against virgin ingot, and is specified for curtain-wall/window sashes — presented this month at a Tokyo Metropolitan Government forum alongside a named, built residential project. Two independent primaries (the manufacturer group's PDF, Tokyo's own forum page) confirm it; one minor, unresolved conflict on the exact completion month. No cert number found for the sash system itself, a smaller gap than for an interior surface given aluminum's non-combustibility. Names a real supplier, 三協立山, for a sustainability-credentialed spec.

Glass just got more expensive for the second time this year, and the increase is already in effect.

Nippon Sheet Glass raised domestic flat-glass/mirror prices 10–20% and functional-glass 10–15% on shipments from October 1, 2026 — the second hike this year after January's 10–25%/20–25% round. NSG cites continuing raw-material, subcontracting, logistics and labor cost increases plus Middle East disruption. A manufacturer wholesale/list-price move, not a single-sale figure, confirmed by two further outlets carrying the release verbatim. Live now — usable in any current glazing-cost conversation.

A September Shinwa Auction sale hammered ukiyo-e and shin-hanga prints at 7 to 13 times their low estimate.

At Shinwa's September 12 近代美術 sale, a Hiroshige went for ¥4,000,000 hammer-plus-premium against a ¥300,000–500,000 estimate; a Kawase Hasui print, ¥1,050,000 against ¥100,000–200,000. The sale totalled ¥223,456,500 hammer-plus-premium at 87.5% clearance. Single-sourced to the house's release, cross-checked against its PR Times original. Practice read: the pre-sale estimate on decorative ukiyo-e/shin-hanga is currently an unreliable budget anchor for guest-room or lobby art.

A Suita restaurant built its whole structure from bark-on logs felled in one named forest, CO2 fixation certified by the prefecture.

江坂ひととき (120.30㎡, Suita) is all-wood: broadleaf logs from a Nose-town forest stand bark-on as columns in a やじろべえ cantilever structure, timber supplied entirely through the Osaka Prefecture forestry cooperative. The architects selected the actual felled trees one by one — not, as an earlier draft had it, picked them standing. Fixes 18.3 tonnes of CO2, certified by Osaka Prefecture, and is built to ZEB standard. Single-sourced, ~2 years old, but the certified-figure disclosure is newly published — a named supply chain plus a quotable sustainability credential for a timber hospitality/F&B commission.

Last dossier

A professional-body proposal to extend architect involvement to small renovations and tenant fit-outs was tabled once, never discussed again, and quietly displaced by an unrelated bill that left every threshold it targeted unchanged. The real exposure for fit-out work is use-change rules attaching liability with no area floor, not the floor-area threshold the proposal argued. Its central claim — that violations are "frequently seen" — is unverifiable from any public dataset. Japan's 2024 fee standard has no renovation/fit-out route at all, by the ministry's own admission; four real interior-only tenders checked against the cost-plus fallback cleared at or above the government's ceiling in three of four cases. Price interior-only fee work assuming the published standard doesn't apply.
