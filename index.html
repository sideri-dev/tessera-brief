MLIT just reset the subcontractor-overhead math under every government construction estimate.

MLIT collapsed its old per-trade overhead-rate table — formwork 18–26%, plastering 19–27%, interior/exterior finishing 15–23%, and so on — into one pair of rates applied across all trades: labor 42–52%, materials 9–13%. Decided in March, it's the base layer underneath both percentage-of-construction-cost fee math and any private subcontractor-overhead negotiation. Worth re-running any overhead assumption against the new pair rather than the old per-trade spread.

Panasonic's own case study names three invisible-layer lighting moves worth stealing.

At Mitsui Garden Hotel Kyoto Sanjo Premier (built by Takenaka for Mitsui Fudosan), the lighting designer suppressed 580nm wavelengths and boosted 600–700nm red in a courtyard spotlight so foliage reads more vivid, built deliberately uneven bathing-area downlights to create darkness rather than uniform illuminance, and scheduled glare-controlled downlights on a timer to cut glass reflections through the day. None of the three needs a published lux or CCT figure to be actionable — the method is the finding, not a number.

Last dossier

A backlog dossier on QE-0005, the only wood-substrate exterior fire certification on record, finds it technically still live — MLIT's own register shows no revocation, and the law creates no expiry mechanism for this class of certificate — but that liveness turns out to be nearly the least useful fact about it. 「外部仕上用」 only strips the gas-emission test from a 準不燃 certificate; it adds nothing for exterior durability, which is exactly why the original holder abandoned the route for six interior-only certificates back in 2014. Nobody can actually be called about it today: the lead holder's registration was closed by the registry in 2022, and the database's listed "current management company" is a different legal entity with no documented link to it. And the certificate was never really the exterior route to begin with — almost nothing in the Building Standards Act requires 準不燃/難燃 performance on an exterior face, so a live QE buys an argument in a fire-code conversation, not a permission.
