Hoshinoya turned a working prison into a 48-suite hotel by joining 9 to 11 cells per room — and a widely repeated delay story about it doesn't check out.

The flagship "10-Cell" suite joins ten former solitary cells: brick exposed where plaster was stripped, a ~3.5m vaulted ceiling, seismic steel columns, added wood panelling. It runs under a concession-style PFI — the Ministry of Justice keeps ownership, a private operator runs it — a transferable route for a protected historic building. Rooms from ¥147,000/night (tax, service included, meals separate); the often-repeated 2024-delay claim doesn't hold up and is dropped.

Last dossier

A ministry manual works a vinyl-tile renovation example in real numbers and settles a live question: when a renovation surcharge (47% labour / 11% material) sits on an already-overhead-inclusive market-rate line, does it stack or damp? It damps — the correction is a ratio with the same rate top and bottom, pulling toward 1 rather than doubling the hit. The underlying correction tables are far larger than assumed (64 worked sheets, already covering plaster, paint and interior finishes), but three cities print different, sometimes outdated versions of the method, and the unit rates stay paywalled. Read: a renovation estimate assuming the full 47%/11% lands as pure added cost is probably overstating it.

This week

Published numbers were the publisher's own curation, on both sides of the job. An auction house's featured "48–132x" spread mixed a hammer-plus-premium figure against a hammer-only estimate; the real numbers lived only in the full, unfeatured lot list — and a city's design-fee "ceiling" read like a construction budget but wasn't. Price from actual catalogue bands, never a featured multiple; check what a "ceiling" actually prices before citing it.

Nobody actually inspects how spray-foam insulation gets covered on site. None of 35 surveyed authorities designate the covering step a checked stage, and renovation sits outside mid-build inspection everywhere checked — while a cost manual and a major vendor both use fire-sounding language that isn't certification. The designer's own drawings and order terms are the only real control left.

Construction supervision keeps turning out to be the fee nobody prices honestly. One city has competitively let it exactly twice in nine years, and a renovation's design fee is routinely capped and awarded before the construction cost exists — so there's never a real ratio to divide by. The defensible number is the national standard's hours-based method, never a percentage of a cost that doesn't exist yet.
