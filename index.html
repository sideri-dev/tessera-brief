Japan's structural glulam imports fell roughly 30% year-on-year in September, and the shortage has a named cause: port delays.

JAWIC's November report (公益財団法人日本木材総合情報センター) shows September structural glulam imports down 31.0% year-on-year in small sections and 29.9% in mid sections, with the lamina (RW4寸材) shortage pinned directly on shipping delays at Busan and Yokohama rather than demand. European Q3 contracts ran around €265/m³, and Q4 pricing is already quoted higher on top of a yen-weakness adjustment — a dated cost input worth pricing into any Q4 CLT/glulam quote now.

Malaysia's largest shipper sent no October shipments at all.

A 19-year-old Yokosuka museum reopened with its halogen gallery lights swapped for LED — specifically to cut UV/IR exposure on the art, not for cost.

Yamamoto Riken's 2007 Yokosuka Museum of Art reopened in September 2026 after a 10-month systems renovation. The display-room conversion from halogen to LED is stated explicitly as an artwork-protection move — lower ultraviolet and infrared load — alongside an HVAC replacement and repairs to salt-air corrosion on the building's eaves, railings and glass-door frames.

A named-architect rationale for an LED retrofit driven by conservation need, not energy cost — directly reusable in a cultural or retail-display lighting argument.

Last dossier

Japanese public contracting does carry a tableware-replenishment provision, but it lives in the kitchen-operations contract, not the goods contract — 高知市's 2026 five-year 給食調理 委託 assigns planned 食器 replenishment and breakage-cost negotiation to the municipality, while the dishes themselves (ordinary ABS, not craft) are bought separately each year as a catalogue purchase. No procurement document specifies a lacquerware re-finish (塗り直し) cycle or a per-piece rate against new supply, for structural legal reasons: multi-year service contracts are capped to an ordinance-enumerated list that excludes goods purchase/repair, and a small repair job clearing the 随意契約 threshold leaves no published bid trail. Where a re-lacquering cycle does exist, it survives only as an informal relationship — pupils re-lacquering their own trays yearly, a ryokan's six-monthly rotation with a named craftsperson, a now-suspended 輪島 union youth-training project — never as a priced contract term. 産地 cities buy their own craft tableware as ordinary annual merchant goods, with the local 組合 upstream of, and never inside, the purchase contract. The governing engineering constraint — rack/rim contact wear, not water or heat — is corroborated across three decades of studies, yet one city adopted lacquerware for daily school service a decade after its own commissioned study reportedly advised against exactly that use.

This week

Where price weight goes to zero, the ceiling is landed to the yen.

Negotiated/sole-source routes (プロポ→随契) settle at 99.6–100% of the published guide figure; price-competed routes settle near 81%. The guide figure can be built on a different basis (construction value) than what the bid must justify (day-rate staffing), and at fit-out scale 監理 exists only if the contract writes it in — a court read an under-priced "設計監理" lump sum as evidence against an actual supervision agreement. Split 設計 and 監理 into separate priced lines; treat any non-price-scored guide figure as the effective fee, not a ceiling to negotiate under.

Certification is per-SKU and per-substrate, not per-catalogue or per-brand.

A wallpaper book's "47% certified" share and a hardware brand's own claims both dissolve once the actual SKU is checked, and a built Osaka restaurant's washi-on-aluminium-honeycomb screen appears to have no numbered certification route at all — no fire-register substrate class covers aluminium beneath washi. Pull the 認定番号 for the exact SKU/substrate/method pairing before specifying, never from a catalogue ratio or brand name.

National data now backs individual craft and material capacity stories at both scales.

Certified 伝統工芸士 exam passers fell from 100 to 88 year-on-year, and Japan's CLT output runs at roughly 3.8% of its own 500,000m³ target — while this week's glulam-import figures show the recommended fallback is itself contracting. Before committing design volume to CLT or glulam, get current, dated lead times for both the primary material and its fallback.
