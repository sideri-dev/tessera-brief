Norm Architects' new Dynaudio flagship in Copenhagen is built entirely around how it sounds.

Eelgrass ceiling panels from Danish maker Søuld do double duty on acoustics and humidity control, cork volumes act as vibration buffers between the speakers and their aluminium plinths, and textile murals use recycled Kvadrat fabric — a permanent, three-floor showroom treating acoustic material as an architectural system rather than an add-on. It extends the same Karimoku/Norm Architects/Keiji Ashizawa furniture relationship up to room scale. Confirmed independently on two non-syndicating publishers.

Mitsui Garden Hotel Sapporo's reopening mills its new reception counter from the operator's own Hokkaido forest holding.

Thinnings off roughly 5,000 hectares the Mitsui Fudosan group owns in Hokkaido went into the reception counter and much of the lobby and guestroom woodwork — a disclosed, vertically integrated timber chain rather than graded open-market lumber. Rooms were redesigned into seven types across 177 rooms, with three-to-four-person rooms expanded from 20 to 140. The timber species itself isn't named in the release — a real gap in the source, not an error — but the traceable-sourcing argument stands on its own against open-market lumber.
