A Toyama farmhouse renovation gives a complete envelope decision, cost to fee, in one case.

発酵カフェループ+, a 1956-built (築68年) farmhouse converted to a café/restaurant, cut its first-floor footprint roughly in half — from 186㎡ to 98㎡ — while the second floor's soil walls (土壁) were deliberately kept outside the seismic-retrofit scope. Thermal performance moved from a UA value of 4.38 W/㎡K to 0.51 W/㎡K on the renovated first floor; the seismic score (上部構造評点Iw) rose from 0.30 to 1.42.

Construction cost (tax-included) was ¥37,000,000, against a design fee (tax-included) of ¥2,200,000 — about 5.9% of construction cost — and a ¥20,000,000 subsidy (事業再構築補助金). It's the one item this cycle with a full retained-vs-upgraded envelope call and a usable fee-to-construction ratio, none of it computed after the fact.

Design-firm bankruptcies just hit a 14-year high, and the mechanism is named.

Tokyo Shoko Research counts 40 「デザイン業」 bankruptcies in H1 2026, up 166.6% year-on-year and the first time the count has topped 40 since 45 in the same period of 2012. Debt tiers: 2 cases at ¥100 million or more (zero the year before), 8 cases between ¥50–100 million (up 300%), 30 cases between ¥10–50 million (up 130.7%); 90% of the failed firms had under 5 employees.

TSR names the cause directly: a surge in housing-maker (wood-frame construction) bankruptcies is cascading downstream into interior-design-focused subcontractors. Worth reading straight into payment-term and deposit-schedule decisions with small interior vendors right now.

The fabricator behind last week's 3D-print concrete ruling just raised ¥1.1 billion and set a 1,000-site target.

Polyuse secured ¥1.1 billion in unsecured, unguaranteed debt from Mizuho Bank and Shoko Chukin Bank, taking cumulative funding to about ¥5 billion. Its print bed runs up to 3000×1900×2500mm; the mortar reaches 30N/mm² at 3 days and 60N/mm² at 28 days; 58 construction methods were applicable as of March 2025; the company targets 1,000 cumulative installations by 2028.

Partner Nishio Rent All has rented the equipment since November 2025, with a live demo on July 23, 2026. Structural glulam imports are down sharply, and domestic mills are eating the cost rather than raising prices — for now.

JAWIC's August 2026 report puts June's structural-glulam imports at 19.4 thousand ㎥ for small cross-sections (down 29.2% year-on-year) and 15.0 thousand ㎥ for medium (down 10.3%). Domestic glulam makers are absorbing rising lamina costs cautiously rather than passing them straight through, with Q3 contracts expected to land around a €10/㎥ increase.

Plywood production rose to 232,000㎥ in June (113.6% of the prior month), but both major eastern and western makers have announced another price rise for September, tied to planned August plant shutdowns. Worth timing a structural glulam spec lock before the price-resistance gap closes.
