Bedding's 防炎 test method changes July 2026 — any label issued before then was tested under a now-superseded method.

The Japan Fire Retardant Association is switching the flame-retardancy test for finished bedding fabrics and futon products from the 45° メセナミン法 to the 45° エアーミックスバーナー金網法, effective July 1, 2026. The pass/fail numbers don't move — carbonized length still caps at 10.0cm max / 8.0cm average — only the test rig changes, fixing a reproducibility problem with the old burner's tendency to shift position.

Confirmed independently by JFRA (the issuing body) and ボーケン (an independent certifier). Practical effect: any 防炎ラベル or 試験番号 on bedding textiles predates a different test unless it was issued on or after July 1 — worth confirming before relying on an existing certification for a live spec.

下請法 becomes 取適法 on January 1, 2026, and it names architectural and interior design drawings explicitly.

The Fair Trade Commission's own guidance confirms that a construction company subcontracting building design, interior design, or drawing production to another firm falls under 情報成果物作成委託 — meaning the new law's protections apply directly to design-drawing work. It adds an employee-count threshold alongside the existing capital threshold: 100 employees for the 情報成果物作成委託・役務提供委託 category that covers design drawings (300 employees is a separate, unrelated threshold for manufacturing subcontracting — don't conflate the two).

It also names a specific prohibited act: unilaterally setting a price without engaging in consultation. That clause is a citable, government-backed argument against a client or general contractor who tries to set a fee without negotiation.

Government daily rates for design technicians jumped 12.6% over two years.

MAFF's own FY2026 procurement table sets the 主任技術者 (lead engineer) 基準日額 at ¥90,300, up from ¥80,200 in the March 2024 table — a straight +12.6% move, both figures from government-primary sources.

This is the reference rate used in public and quasi-public fee negotiations; if a live engagement's rate was set before March 2026, this is dated leverage to revisit it.

LIXIL's tile and エコカラット prices are moving 11-13% from August 3, with two more increases staggered through October.

LIXIL's own price notice: floor tile up about 12%, interior wall tile about 13%, エコカラット about 11%, exterior wall tile about 11%, and installation materials/tools about 20% — all effective for orders from August 3, 2026.

A second wave (metal/resin siding, roofing) lands September 1, and interior building materials (fittings, flooring) move 12-15% from October 1. Three separate effective dates across categories — worth checking which applies before quoting against LIXIL's catalogue.

SBI Art Auction's July sale put fresh numbers on mid-career contemporary Japanese secondary-market pricing.

The July 10-11 sale cleared ¥657,087,000 total at an 86.9% clearance rate, hammer-plus-premium, tax excluded (SBI's own labelling). Notable multiples over estimate: a Yoshitomo Nara painting estimated at ¥30-50 million hammered at ¥52.9 million; a Takashi Murakami piece estimated ¥5-8 million hammered at ¥29.9 million — roughly 4x; an Aya Rokkaku piece estimated ¥7-13 million hammered at ¥32.2 million.

Useful, correctly-typed comparators for art-procurement budget conversations on hospitality installations. The sale is about six and a half weeks old — read as a historical clearing price, not a claim about current value.
