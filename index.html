A Princeton structural study just put a number on why CNC-cutting a traditional joint isn't the same as hand-cutting it.

A 2026 ISARC paper from Princeton CEE built and structurally tested the daimochi tsugi — a shear-resistant scarf joint used in temple and shrine roofs since the Kamakura period — both by hand and by CNC: hand-cut joints held roughly 40% higher peak load, the gap coming down to fabrication precision and contact quality. Single-source but the paper is its own primary, and there's no 建築基準法 certification route yet for this pairing — worth having ready next time a contractor proposes CNC-cutting a traditional 継手 to save time on load-bearing work.

This week

The register is not the certificate.

Six 認定番号 for sprayed urethane fire-protection were formally revoked, but 46 superseded or pending numbers still read live in MLIT's own lookup with no supersession signal — binding conditions live only in attachments the register never publishes. The same invisible layer decided a real façade (Bato Hiroshige Museum's cedar-to-aluminium swap was a certification problem, not durability), so the register alone is no longer enough to check.

Renovation is the practice's real market, and nothing prices it properly.

84% of public design commissions by count are renovation, yet the ministry's fee standard has no renovation route — the real instrument hides in a construction-costing manual whose 2024 revision quietly cut small-job fees despite a headline rate increase. Basic design and as-built surveys bill separately and are easy to miss, renovation supervision has no formula at all, and that gap — a lifecycle renewal plan with access-method pricing — is a scope line worth charging for.

Capacity collapse is being answered with vertical integration.

A 230-year-old Echizen lacquerware workshop is bringing the substrate stage in-house because only two such workshops remain nationally; an Arita kiln has lost 70% of its craftspeople in under a decade; one hotel operator now sources its own millwork timber from ~5,000 hectares of owned forest. Lead-time risk concentrates at the thinnest surviving stage regardless of which workshop takes the order — worth asking any 産地 contact which stages they actually hold in-house.
