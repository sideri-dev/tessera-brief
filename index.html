Odawara's lacquerware supply has collapsed to a structural floor, not a soft decline — and the one person holding most of what's left has no successor.

神奈川新聞カナロコ's 産地 report on 小田原漆器 confirms cheap mass tableware has cut operating workshops from 13 to 4 and production value from over ¥400 million a year to the low ¥20-millions — about 1/20th of peak. The 伝統小田原漆器組合's own chairman personally employs more than 60 木地師 (wood-turners) through the union and has no named successor to his own role.

Don't assume Odawara lacquerware as standing-stock craft supply for a hospitality bill of materials without naming a specific surviving 工房 and checking its lead time directly — the capacity is thinner than the craft's reputation suggests, and the leadership gap is itself a risk to price in.

A government-funded field study found business-hotel guest rooms miss Japan's own humidity standard most of the time — even with proper central air conditioning.

Across 27 rooms in 7 cities, only 7 (26%) stayed inside the legal relative-humidity band for the entire measured period; the overall non-compliance rate for humidity was 74%, well above temperature's 30%. Many rooms ran over 70% relative humidity in summer and under 40% in winter. Centrally air-conditioned rooms held the band longer than split-system rooms, which swung harder and more often.

If central HVAC with professional engineering still fails this badly, a split-system guestroom spec is the default to interrogate — not assume — on any hospitality renovation brief.

A Gifu research lab quantified exactly how much stronger a twisted-tenon joint is than a square one — and exactly what it costs in machine time.

CNC-cut 捻じれホゾ (twisted-tenon) joints pulled roughly 5× stronger than standard square 角ホゾ tenons on a tensile tester (909.6N average vs. 182.8N), at a machining-time cost of about 3.75× (74m36s vs. 20m15s per joint pairing). The paper frames the work against the 飛騨・高山の家具® 産地 mark.

A tested, quantified joinery upgrade worth specifying wherever joint failure — not material failure — is the actual risk on a CNC-joinery furniture commission.

The question as queued was partly mis-framed: a multi-year municipal design-commission's zeroed next fiscal year is a disbursement schedule under a two-year budget ceiling, not a year genuinely "budgeted at nothing" — no extraordinary mechanism is needed to explain it. A real mechanism does exist, with two branches: an elective partial-payment clause on request, and, where that clause is absent, payment tied to a designated interim deliverable dated to fiscal year-end — a ¥1.63 billion Kumamoto city-hall commission shows the second at work. One city's own contract form and tender pack were checked clause-by-clause and found to omit partial-payment language entirely, settling that route as switched off there and leaving advance payment undetermined. Authorities disagree on what base a multi-year advance is calculated against — the whole contract, or just that fiscal year's slice — and that disagreement matters more to a designer's cash-flow planning than any single figure in the dossier. One upstream question — which fiscal year a design-commission fee legally belongs to — is presented as an open fork, not resolved.
