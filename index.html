Japan's own ministry just told the market the formaldehyde test behind every F☆☆☆☆ plywood spec is up for revision.

MAFF opened a public-comment window (令和8年11月5日–令和8年12月4日) on a brand-new, stand-alone JAS standard for how 合板等のホルムアルデヒド放散量 (plywood-class formaldehyde emissions) gets tested — not a tweak to an existing method, a new one. No certification number exists yet, and the draft test-method text itself wasn't reached this cycle.

What's confirmed is that the testing basis every interior plywood spec leans on is in active, multi-instrument revision — the certification-route risk itself, not a story about one.

Osaka just named the exact date — 令和7年3月6日 — its own fee-calculation standard caught up with the national one, closing a gap three dossiers here had already flagged as unreachable.

The prefecture's own page confirms it revised its 設計監理業務委託料算定基準 after 告示第8号 took national effect in January 2024, but applied the new basis only to RFPs publicly announced from 令和7年3月6日 onward — over a year after the national switch. No trace of the superseded 告示第98号 remains, and no transition language softens the cutoff.

Any Osaka tender publicly announced before that date risked calculation on a standard already dead everywhere else in the country.

The Bank of Japan's own October price index shows domestic timber costs still accelerating — and a corrected reading of the import side points the other way than first read.

The central bank's Corporate Goods Price Index puts domestic 木材・木製品 at +9.0% year-on-year for October, up from +8.5% in September — continued acceleration, not a plateau. The import side needed a correction: a submitted figure mixed up a month-on-month column with the year-on-year one.

Read correctly, imported timber's contract-currency year-on-year change is +2.9% (yen-basis +6.3%, down from September's revised +7.6%) — a smaller swing than first claimed, and worth checking before quoting either number in a timber-heavy BOQ conversation this month.

A Fukuoka building just opened structural tours for a pure-wood, fire-resistant five-storey CLT frame — but neither its fabricator nor its actual fire-certification basis is named anywhere.

Built on a May 2026 timber-sourcing agreement with Fukuoka Prefecture, the five-storey panel-method CLT structure uses 359 cubic metres of prefecture-sourced wood (an estimated 249 tonnes of CO2 storage) and moved from a design-demonstration track to a construction-demonstration one under a national programme.

It's a usable precedent for a regional-timber CLT sourcing argument, but only a precedent: no CLT fabricator is named, and the "fire-resistant" claim carries no specific certification number — just the word "耐火建築物" asserted. Worth chasing before citing it as a spec precedent rather than an argument. The tour's own registration deadline already passed before this page was published, though the tour dates (Nov 17–26) themselves still stand.

Last dossier

提灯 (paper lanterns) turn out not to be a 27th category JFRA certifies outside its own list, as an earlier dossier had suggested — they're a named use case written inside an existing tent/sheet/curtain category, carrying ordinary certification numbers that interleave with tarpaulins in the register. The real finding is narrower and more useful: a certification committee can quietly open a new sub-type inside an existing category without amending the governing rule, and this is the one sub-type that left no trace anywhere else in the scheme's paperwork. In practice it's tiny — seven certificates from two non-member holders across two quarters of 2025, none since — restricted to LED-only lamps, a mandatory protective device, and washi certified for indoor use only. For specification purposes the label stays voluntary: no statute names it, flame-resistance isn't non-combustibility, and no certification route for flame-treated washi could be established.
