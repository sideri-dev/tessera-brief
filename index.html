Last dossier

Japan has no interior noise number or reverberation target for a restaurant or hotel room — the only statute naming reverberation time and absorption coefficient is the fire-alarm rule, where absorption is a compliance input, not a comfort target. Tokyo's own noise ordinance turns out to be the real liability line, not the building code. The certifiable acoustic palette is narrow and dated (1979–2002 mineral wool and gypsum), with no certified PET felt and a hard bind between two separate certification statuses that a wall finish can't satisfy at once.

Absorption work runs roughly 0.4–0.6% of construction cost per square metre against national budget unit prices, while a single acoustic fire door runs 54% over a standard glazed fire-rated door. The strongest field evidence found says louder diners spend more — a genuine argument for restraint in a client room, not just a compliance one.
