Six fire-retardant certifications you might still be specifying from were just revoked, and survivors now carry a new covering rule.

MLIT pulled six 大臣認定 numbers (NM-4964, NM-5345, NM-5385, RM-0013, RM-0015, RM-0016) for sprayed urethane/isocyanurate fire materials, effective this April — those certificates had no production history. More load-bearing: every carried-forward certificate's successor now requires an inorganic, non-exposed covering over the foam (excluding things like aluminium foil, which can melt away under heat), binding on any building breaking ground from October 1, 2026. Check any sprayed-foam spec against MLIT's own list.

Echizen lacquerware's substrate-prep stage is down to two workshops, and the strongest 工房 response to that is worth knowing before commissioning there.

河和田's lacquerware workshops fell from 354 (1998) to 184 (2024), and 下地 — the durability-determining prep stage — is down to just two workshops. 漆琳堂, an eight-generation Echizen lacquer house, is responding with vertical integration (下地・木地・研ぎ in-house) and a capital tie-up with Nakagawa Masashichi Shoten, plus a new Fukasawa Naoto line opening for orders this month. Single-source and dated mid-summer, but the clearest Echizen capacity signal in months.

A derelict quarry-wall building in Utsunomiya just reopened with three reusable moves for adaptive reuse of industrial stone.

Architect Masafumi Harigai converted a 1967 tourist facility built into an Ōya-stone quarry wall — derelict three decades — into a café and gallery, running demolition and design concurrently. An upper floor slab was partly cut to pass light and wind; new glazing meets the stone directly with no sash frame; existing plaster, blackened steel and dyed OSB were kept to age. Three transferable moves: demolition-as-design, a frameless glass-to-stone detail, and floor removal for light rather than program.

US fee-method data gives you a quotable comparator: percentage-of-construction-cost is now a minority-regular practice there.

AIA/Deltek's latest US survey: stipulated sum (fixed fee) used regularly by 73% of firm leaders, professional-fee-plus-reimbursable by 61%; percentage-of-construction-cost regularly by only 24% (occasionally 32%), with cost-not-to-exceed and fee-per-square-foot used at all by barely a quarter to a third of firms. Stipulated sum is also the most-cited most-profitable method. A dated comparator for a fee negotiation: the direction of travel is away from percentage-of-cost toward fixed and reimbursable structures.

Source: AIA/Deltek Architecture Billings Index, January 2026 special practice questions, published February 18, 2026.

A renovated Oki Islands hotel shows how to pull a sea view out of an existing window opening without touching the envelope.

AMANE ARCHITECTS renovated an existing concrete hotel on Nishinoshima, keeping the frame and holding back new decoration. In the best room, they removed the window frame entirely and raised the bed about a metre so the sea view holds all day; operable north/south windows add cross-ventilation; finishes are solid Oki cedar and rattan, chosen to soften light and make airflow visible. A clean move: frame removal plus a raised platform to extract a view from an existing opening.

A backlog follow-up on Kuma Kengo's exterior-timber record finds the pattern thinner than it looked. 雲の上のホテル has no documented timber-failure cause on file — it's a whole-asset renewal on legal depreciation life, weakening rather than supporting the claim. 那須歴史探訪館 is a genuine component case: a 2024 roof-ornament repair contract cleared at ¥2,728,000 after three years of budget records naming 屋根材の剥離. 浅草文化観光センター is the harder case — vertical cedar-composite louvers took a ¥81,447,000 repair at year 7, replacing only the most-exposed units. Lesson: exposure decides whether repair happens, reachability decides the cost.

Which register a 公告 names decides whether an unlicensed interior practice is admitted at all.

Exhibition/interior scope on a goods-and-services register skips the 一級建築士事務所 gate entirely, while most public new-build design is price-bid, not proposal-judged. 監修/design-credit work is a nameless contract in zero statutes; a first-stage competition entry now costs a measured ~40 person-days against a few-percent chance of a token honorarium.

The binding constraint sits one level below the headline instrument.

At Imperial Hotel Kyoto, national heritage registration couldn't stop 80% of the footprint being removed — the municipal height/landscape overlay did the real constraining. At Kansai Airport T1, the terminal has no statutory "use," so certification burden falls on the tenant shops, not the shell.

This autumn's price wave is a supply shock turning into a stale estimate, line by line.

Nankai Plywood's "flat 10%" rise is true only in aggregate — lines actually move +7% to +17%, and since the step equals the consumption tax rate, mixed tax-in/tax-out schedules can hide it entirely. Re-check any pre-summer plywood, glass-wool or glulam estimate against the vendor's own old/new list.
