Shiga Prefecture just named a craft with exactly one maker and one apprentice as a protected tradition — and no backup supplier exists.

木籠, a rare wood-strip basketry technique, was designated a Shiga prefectural traditional craft at a ceremony on April 30, 2026. The prefecture's own page names the maker, 太々野㓛さん, as the sole practitioner from 小原村, and his one apprentice, 荒井恵梨子さん, as training under him since 2018. Single-sourced to the government page; an unverifiable claimed designation date was dropped rather than carried. The entire supply base for this craft object, in one sentence: one maker, one successor, zero alternates — worth knowing before quoting a lead time.

A Ginza hostel became a one-suite-per-floor boutique hotel, and the furniture credit names three suppliers together.

TOMI Ginza, converted by Keiji Ashizawa Design in Shintomicho, Tokyo, turned a ten-floor hostel that once packed multiple rooms onto each floor into a building with just one self-contained suite per floor — standard suites get a living area and two bedrooms, only the penthouse drops to one. Karimoku Case's new Dynaudio Lounge furniture line makes its joinery the visible finish, not something to hide.

The sofa, coffee table and stool launching this winter under Karimoku Case's CASE 13 line (Norm Architects, for the already-published Dynaudio Lounge in Copenhagen) deliberately expose their joints as the design feature — the table's corner joints show the piece's "structural beauty as it is," and the stool's backrest joins are "intentionally visualized" rather than concealed. The maker pitches the stool explicitly for hotel lounges. Single-sourced on the launch facts; the building context is already corroborated in corpus. A repeatable, named construction-as-ornament method on a line already positioned for hospitality.

A recycled textile-and-wood board is going up as a 25-metre installation at Tokyo's design fair — with no fire certification in sight.

PANECO® board M, 51% waste clothing and 49% waste wood on an MDF line, has been commercially available since mid-July 2026 and is now built into a 25-metre structure ("RE:FIT") for DESIGNART TOKYO 2026, Oct 30–Nov 3. Two independent releases confirm the composition, dimensions (1,240×1,850mm and 1,240×2,450mm, 9/12/15mm) and full circularity — it re-grinds back into board after use. A targeted search of the spec page returns zero hits for any Japanese fire-certification marker (不燃/準不燃/難燃/防炎/F☆/JIS/認定番号). A lead worth tracking, not yet a material to specify for an interior — the gap is real, and price/lead time are inquiry-only.

Last dossier

A roof repair that replaces a joint in an existing non-conforming timber building gets swept into full seismic relief under Order Article 137-12 — the structural drawing set collapses to one seismic-diagnosis report, and the joinery standard never becomes a submission item. A rule effective November 2025 carves fire status back out of that relief: touching the roof forfeits its fire-zone exemption even while everything else stays relieved. No ministry document defines how a partial splice counts toward the "majority repair" threshold that triggers any of this, and the only public instances found — three Kyoto hospitality conversions — show the joinery was always exempted outright, never tested. Practice read: a renovation quote assuming the joinery needs recalculation is probably over-scoped, but a roof-touching renovation needs its own fire-material check regardless.
